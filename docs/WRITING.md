# How NovelCheck talks

Short, plain, American English. The reader is a parent on a phone, not an engineer.

## Rules

1. **Lead with what to do or what happened.** "Delete the ticked books" beats "The ticked books can be deleted from the Kindle at its next sync".
2. **One idea per sentence, about 20 words at most.** A hint under a field is one sentence.
3. **Buttons are 1 to 4 words**, a verb first: "Back up", "Send", "Keep this copy".
4. **No parentheses inside sentences** unless it is a short example. Never two in one sentence.
5. **Say it once.** If the button says "Delete the ticked books", the text above it doesn't repeat it.
6. **Skip how it works inside.** "It happens at the Kindle's next sync" is enough; how often the plugin checks belongs in the help, not on the card.
7. **Warnings name the risk in a few words**: "Can't be undone." "Kindle-store books won't show in Amazon's reader."
8. **Numbers as digits**, times as "15 minutes", not "a quarter of an hour".
9. **The same thing has the same name everywhere**: Nightstand, Library, Library cleanup, Kindle remote, plugin.
10. **Long help goes in the guide or a details box**, not on the main card.

## Before and after

- Before: "Things you ask for here happen the next time the Kindle is online (the plugin looks every few minutes while it is connected, or press NovelCheck > Do the tasks from NovelCheck now on the Kindle). Deleting only works for books NovelCheck holds a copy of."
- After: "Your Kindle does these at its next sync. Only backed-up books can be deleted."
